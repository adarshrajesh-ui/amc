Candidate background 
 I previously worked at Zupee as an SDE (I joined as an SDET) for a little over a year. 
 Interview process overview 
 The process had four stages: an online screening test, three technical rounds, and an HR/culture fit discussion. 
 1) Initial screening (HackerEarth) 
 The first step was a ≈2-hour HackerEarth assessment with a mix of MCQs and coding questions. The questions covered Python, Bash, and general problem-solving. 
 2) Technical rounds 
 Round 1 (SDE-2) 
 This round focused heavily on my current role, past work, resume, and projects. Alongside that, I was asked a few questions on DSA and Python fundamentals. Topics included: 
 Python internals and concepts like the GIL and monkey patching 
 Basic DSA questions in Python 
 Some basic Bash commands and usage 
 Overall, it felt like a “depth check” on what I’ve actually worked on, plus a baseline test of fundamentals. 
 Round 2 (SDE-2) 
 This round leaned much more into Bash and practical problem-solving. I had mentioned upfront that Bash isn’t something I use daily, and the questions reflected that—the focus was more on conceptual clarity and approach rather than memorizing commands. 
 I was asked about tools/commands and scenarios involving: 
 awk , grep, sudo, mkdir, touch, cat, cp, wget, chmod , etc. 
 Solving the same problem using multiple approaches (they expected alternatives and trade-offs) 
 There were also Python DSA questions, including time and space complexity discussions and how to optimize solutions. 
 A big part of this round was real-world debugging: interpreting logs, spotting errors, and solving “production-like” scenarios. For example, one question explored how to find the exact timestamp where an error began in the minimum number of steps—essentially applying binary search thinking to logs. There were also discussions around practical uses of hash maps, arrays, and strings. 
 Round 3 (Principal Architect) 
 This round started with a discussion on my current responsibilities, tech stack, and what I understood about the role. 
 It then moved into broader engineering topics: 
 Debugging practices and how I approach issues systematically 
 Infrastructure-level conversations around Kubernetes, Docker, and Devtron 
 Design-oriented scenarios: given a feature, how I’d approach it with edge cases, future scalability, and maintainability in mind 
 Collaboration and communication: how I would align with team leads, PMs, managers, and other stakeholders 
 The key technical task in this round was to design and code a schema validator. Requirements evolved during the discussion, and I was expected to adapt the solution on the fly. I understood the scenarios but found it tricky to implement everything perfectly within the time window, so I delivered a partially working solution during the interview. After the round, with more time, I was able to complete a fully working version. 
 3) Round 4 (HR / culture fit) 
 This was a culture-fit round focused on my background, expectations from the role, and overall alignment with the team and organization. It concluded with the usual employment-related discussions. 
 Post-interview reflections 
 I had interviewed for two other roles in the same organization earlier: SDE Tech Tools and SDE Quant Tools. Getting into this HFT was something I had been aiming for for a while. 
 Previously, I reached the final round in both interview loops but didn’t make it through for the Quant Tools role, and I narrowly missed the offer for the Tech Tools role (as I learned later). I stayed persistent, kept improving, and fortunately succeeded this time. 
 Closing note 
 Overall, the process was intense but genuinely exciting, and I learned something in every round. I also made it a point to ask each interviewer about their role and projects, which helped me understand the breadth of work happening across the organization. 
 I faced rejections here (and at other companies) along the way, but consistency really does pay off. I also interviewed at multiple places—Amazon, INDMoney, SAFE Security, myKaarma, Lyzer, and several early-stage startups—and cleared a few processes. That said, I’m grateful to have landed in a role I genuinely wanted. 
 Hope this was helpful, Best of Luck!
